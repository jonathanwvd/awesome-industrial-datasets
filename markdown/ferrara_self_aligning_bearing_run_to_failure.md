# Run-to-failure vibration dataset of self-aligning double-row ball bearings

Radial acceleration over six accelerated run-to-failure tests of self-aligning double-row ball bearings at the University of Ferrara, published in two parts.

- **Domain:** Manufacturing
- **Asset / Process:** Bearings
- **Modality:** Vibration
- **Task:** RUL and prognostics
- **Annotation:** RUL label
- **Source Type:** Lab or testbed
- **Access:** Mendeley Data
- **Size:** 6 run-to-failure tests
- **Publisher:** University of Ferrara

## Description

This dataset from the Department of Engineering of the University of Ferrara follows the vibration of self-aligning double-row rolling element bearings over their whole life. It comes from an experimental campaign of six accelerated run-to-failure tests and is split into two Mendeley Data records, Part 1 and Part 2.

On the test rig, an inverter-controlled electric motor turns the shaft at constant speed and a lever system applies a constant radial load, set through a load cell, to a 1205 ETN 9 bearing mounted at the shaft end. A uniaxial PCB 353B15 accelerometer on the bearing housing records the radial acceleration through an NI cRIO controller with a 9234 module.

Every 5 minutes, 5 s of signal are stored at 25.6 kHz, as .mat files that each hold the radial acceleration signal and its sampling frequency. Each test stops when the acceleration peak reaches 20 g, and every bearing ends with an extended outer raceway defect. The publishers place it in machine diagnostics and prognostics research, where estimating remaining useful life is a central aim.
