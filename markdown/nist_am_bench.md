# Additive Manufacturing Benchmark Test Series (AM-Bench)

NIST-led series of highly controlled additive manufacturing measurements, archived for public use, against which modelers test their process simulations.

- **Domain:** Manufacturing
- **Asset / Process:** Additive manufacturing
- **Modality:** Multimodal
- **Task:** Other
- **Source Type:** Lab or testbed
- **Access:** Official portal
- **Year:** 2018
- **Publisher:** National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST)

## Description

The Additive Manufacturing Benchmark Test Series (AM-Bench) is a continuing program led by NIST that produces highly controlled benchmark measurements for additive manufacturing (AM), poses modeling challenge problems and holds conferences. Its aim is to give modelers rigorous measurement data for testing their simulations, and all AM-Bench data are archived for public use in dedicated data management systems.

Rounds were completed in 2018 and 2022, with the 2025 round finishing its final data publications. Metal sets centre on laser powder bed fusion (PBF-LB) of superalloys 625 and 718 and 15-5 stainless steel, and later sets add composition sensitivity, high-cycle fatigue and laser hot-wire DED. Polymer sets cover material extrusion of polycarbonate, PBF-LB of polyamide 12 and vat photopolymerization. Measurements range from feedstock and in situ build data to 2D and 3D microstructure, residual strains and mechanical behavior.

In blind challenge problems, modelers predict the measured results. On a three-year cycle, results are discussed at a conference, with proceedings in Integrating Materials and Manufacturing Innovation (IMMI).
