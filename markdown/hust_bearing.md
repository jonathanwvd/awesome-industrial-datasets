# HUST bearing

Vibration signals of ball bearings with six artificial crack types on five bearing models and three loads, from a project funded by Hanoi University of Science and Technology.

- **Domain:** Manufacturing
- **Asset / Process:** Bearings
- **Modality:** Vibration
- **Task:** Fault diagnosis
- **Annotation:** Class label
- **Source Type:** Lab or testbed
- **Access:** Mendeley Data
- **Size:** 99 raw vibration signals
- **Year:** 2023

## Description

HUST bearing is a vibration dataset for diagnosing faults in ball bearings, described in a 2023 data note in BMC Research Notes. The work was funded by Hanoi University of Science and Technology. Signals come from a test bench in which a 750 W induction motor drives a multi-step shaft, with a powder brake acting as the load.

The set holds 99 raw vibration signals. Defects were made artificially: inner, outer and ball cracks and their pairwise combinations, six types in all, on five ball bearing models (6204 to 6208) at loads of 0 W, 200 W and 400 W. Each signal lasts 10 s at 51,200 samples per second. A further 30 recordings capture defective bearings during a 5 s run-up.

The data is hosted on Mendeley Data. Crack depth differs between bearings, so signal amplitudes vary markedly between cases, and environmental noise is not part of the recordings.
