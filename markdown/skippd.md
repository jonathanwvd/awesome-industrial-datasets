# 2017-2019 Sky Images and Photovoltaic Power Generation Dataset for Short-term Solar Forecasting (Stanford Benchmark)

Three years of 64x64 fish-eye sky images paired with 1-minute power output of a 30-kW rooftop PV array at Stanford, for short-term solar forecasting.

- **Domain:** Energy and power
- **Asset / Process:** Solar PV
- **Modality:** Image; Time series
- **Task:** Forecasting
- **Source Type:** Real production
- **Access:** Official portal
- **Size:** 3 years of 1-minute images and PV output
- **Year:** 2022
- **License:** CC BY
- **Publisher:** Stanford University

## Description

This dataset from Stanford University pairs sky images with photovoltaic power output for short-term solar forecasting, a way to anticipate the fluctuations that make solar power hard to integrate into electricity grids. A team headed by Adam Brandt published it in the Stanford Digital Repository in June 2022.

It covers 3 years, 2017 to 2019. A ground-based fish-eye camera supplies processed sky images down-sampled to 64x64, and a 30-kW rooftop PV array about 125 meters from the camera on the Stanford Campus supplies power output; both streams are logged once per minute. High-resolution raw data for the same years are offered as related items.

The authors prepared the data for deep-learning solar forecasting and hope it helps form a standardized benchmark for comparing forecasting models. They also suggest cloud classification, cloud image segmentation and cloud movement forecasting as further uses. The license is CC BY 4.0.
